What changed, and why

A viewer toolbar that belongs to the viewer. View transform, channel isolation, viewer exposure and compare mode sit above the image where Nuke, Resolve and RV put them. Viewer exposure is not the grade: it moves what you are looking at without touching what gets written.

Four compare modes instead of one. Wipe with a draggable handle, A/B flip on hold, split, and difference. The current build has hold-to-flip only, which is the one mode that cannot show you a soft edge.

A probe that reads in nits. RGB in cd/m², stops over diffuse white and pixel coordinates under the cursor. For HDR work this is the single most-used readout and the current UI has no equivalent.

A real timeline. Per-frame cache state, in and out, scrub, loop and a Render sequence button. The pending "master the whole sequence in one press" job lives here rather than in a menu.

Deliver is a panel, not a button. Six targets, MaxCLL and MaxFALL shown as measured from the frames, the colour-tag verification result, and a queue with progress. Everything rudra deliver can already do, surfaced.

Simple and Full workspaces. Simple hides the scopes dock and the advanced groups, leaving open, look, compare, render. Full is the TD view. One switch, top right; nothing is buried where an artist cannot find it.

Progressive disclosure in the inspector. Three tabs rather than one long column, advanced groups collapsed by default, and every measured number kept out of the controls and put in the scopes dock where it belongs.

The gate is visible. The shadow gate's per-frame weight is shown and explained in one line, because a model that silently decides how much to do is a model an artist will not trust.

Neutral chrome. No saturated colour anywhere near the image except where it means something: amber and red are reserved for clipping and failure, so a warning cannot be mistaken for decoration.

Still to decide

  • False colour scale. The overlay here is illustrative. Real false colour needs a fixed nit-to-hue mapping with a printed legend, and the choice of scale is a house decision: ARRI, Rec.2100 reference, or your own.
  • Where masks live. Highlight and shadow masks are currently implicit. They could be a viewer overlay, a channel in the channel picker, or their own tab. The rail has a slot for them.
  • Playback. A 1920×1080 sequence at 24 fps needs the cache ahead of the playhead. The cache bar assumes a background render; if that is not wanted, playback becomes scrub-only and the bar goes away.
