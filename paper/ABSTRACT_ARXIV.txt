We treat single-image inverse tone mapping as a question about information
rather than about model capacity. A compact residual model on an analytic
inverse tone map is scored against that same analytic map on 429 held-out
frames, in PU21-PSNR and in ColorVideoVDP (CVVDP) JOD. On degraded input, the condition
real footage arrives in, it gains 1.43 dB and 0.44 JOD on 348 of 429 frames. On clean, well-graded input it loses 3.0 dB of PU21-PSNR for
a perceptually invisible -0.046 JOD, the two metrics disagreeing sharply about
the practical significance of the same difference, which is itself one of our
results. The regression is carried by low-dynamic-range frames: the model has no
representation of how much headroom a frame has, so it cannot decide to do
nothing.

We then measure what repairing that is worth and whether it can be repaired. An
oracle per-frame scale on the residual is worth +5.84 dB on clean and +0.29 dB
on degraded input at the same time, and no constant achieves it. But a linear
readout of the features such a gate would see explains 3% of that scale's
variance, and 79% of the variance lies within a condition rather than between
conditions, so even a perfect clean-versus-degraded classifier is capped at
R^2 = 0.213. Capacity, corpus and objective were each varied by a factor of four
to six and none moved the result. We report this as an empirical identifiability
bound on the representations we tested, not an information-theoretic one.

That bound is a property of the question. Posed as a continuous per-frame scale
it is unreachable; posed as a binary decision on the one axis that is
detectable, it is reachable. A 21,121-parameter gate on the shadow arm of the
reconstruction converts the clean regression into a gain of 3.41 +/- 0.32 dB and
0.135 +/- 0.023 JOD over the shipped model, for 0.30 +/- 0.15 dB and
0.091 +/- 0.041 JOD given up on degraded input, across three seeds.
