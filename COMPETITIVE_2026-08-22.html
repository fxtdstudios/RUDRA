FXTD Studios · Radiance Research Competitive assessment 22 August 2026

Ruby, Hyperion,
RUDRA

Two commercial SDR → HDR products shipped in the same four-day window. Neither of them does what RUDRA does — but both of them ship, and RUDRA currently does not. Here is the honest scoreboard and the one position worth defending.

00

The enhanced prompt

You asked me to sharpen the brief before answering it. This is the version I ran against — it is worth keeping as the template for the next one of these.

Brief as executed

Act as a technical competitive analyst for an HDR imaging product team.

  1. Establish the facts. Verify what Runway shipped as “Ruby” and what Topaz shipped for SDR→HDR in August 2026, from primary sources where possible. Record release date, model name, inputs, output containers and transfer functions, colour space, control surface, hardware/deployment model, and real per-minute cost. Flag anything only confirmable through a third party.
  2. Establish our own position honestly. Read the actual state of the rudra repo and Radiance Studio — not the README's aspirations. Separate what is trained and measured from what is coded and untrained. Treat the outstanding retrain debt as a competitive fact, not a footnote.
  3. Compare hard. One matrix, dimension by dimension: quality evidence, colour-science rigour, formats and metadata, temporal stability, artist control, throughput, cost, deployment, licensing. Mark each row win / loss / tie for us. Do not grade on a curve, and do not compare our decoder-reconstruction numbers against their inverse-tone-mapping numbers — that is a category error.
  4. Find the structural moat. Identify what we can do that a post-hoc pixel tool cannot, for reasons of physics and pipeline order rather than effort. Distinguish it from advantages a well-funded competitor could copy in a quarter.
  5. Recommend, in order. A sequenced plan — credibility first, then differentiation, then distribution — with what to build, what to measure, and explicitly what to stop doing. Every claim tied to a source or marked unverified.

Deliverable: a standalone report I can send to a collaborator, with a comparison matrix and a dated source list.

01

What actually shipped

Four days, two releases, and a third competitor already sitting in the professional lane. The market for “make my SDR footage HDR” went from niche to contested this month.

Runway — Ruby

20 Aug 2026 · cloud only

Converts SDR uploads or Runway generations into HDR10, HLG, ProRes mezzanine, or OpenEXR sequences. Original pixels and audio preserved; brightness extended into HDR headroom.

  • 20 credits/sec, 40 above ~4 MP
  • Landed alongside native HDR output for Gen-4.5 and Aleph 2.0 — “graded into BT.2020 with measured HDR10 metadata, not tone-mapped afterward”
  • No local execution, no weights

Topaz — Hyperion 2.5

18 Aug 2026 · desktop + cloud

Inverse tone mapping with per-frame luminance analysis and learned highlight/shadow reconstruction. 8-bit in, 10-bit ProRes or 16-bit EXR out.

  • BT.2020 PQ (HDR10) and ACES 2065-1
  • Also H.265 Main10, AV1 10-bit, 16-bit PNG, uncompressed QuickTime
  • Fully automatic — no adjustment sliders
  • In Astra now (from $19/mo); Topaz Video “coming soon” ($59/mo)
  • Documented weak on interlaced, very dark or blown sources, and shifting light in motion

Beeble — SwitchHDR

Incumbent · the one to actually beat

Scene-linear 16-bit EXR in ACES 2065-1, trained on real HDR footage, aimed squarely at feature, spot and restoration work.

  • Artist control: luminance masks + text prompts for selective region reconstruction
  • Explicitly positioned against one-button conversion
  • Residual temporal instability on complex texture

Academic SOTA

arXiv 2605.14703 · the measuring stick

Exposure-bracket generation (MEVM) plus a learned merge (VMM), beating LumiVid on perceptual metrics.

  • CVVDP 6.56 Stuttgart (over-exposed), 8.91 UBC (under-exposed)
  • PU-PSNR 27.60 / 26.84
  • Benchmarks: Stuttgart HDR (26 videos, 11,336 sequences) and UBC

The part of the Runway release that matters most

Ruby is the headline, but it is not the threat. The threat is the line item next to it: Gen-4.5 and Aleph 2.0 now render natively into BT.2020 with measured HDR10 metadata rather than tone-mapping after the fact. That is Runway building, inside their own walled model, the exact architectural claim RUDRA was built to own — HDR that never round-trips through SDR. They have it for one model family. RUDRA has it for seven open backbones. That gap is the entire business.

03

Where we lose, without softening it

Five of these are fixable in a quarter. The first one is fixable this week and blocks the other four.

01

Nothing currently shipped is trained on correct data

The flip-augmentation bug corrupted roughly half of every offline epoch, and the ingest EOTFs decoded PQ ten times dark. Both are fixed in code; neither is fixed in any weight on Hugging Face. Until the re-ingest → re-pair → retrain sequence completes, our real quality is unknown and our public model card is describing a model that no longer exists.

02

The core thesis is untrained

Stage 3 — the DRE transformer with cross-attention injection, the actual paper contribution — has only ever been dry-run. Stage 2 stopped at 400 steps on an OOM. What is trained and measured is Stage 1, the descriptor plus FiLM decoder. The JOD 9.0–9.2 number belongs to that, on latent reconstruction, and it must never be quoted next to an inverse-tone-mapping result.

03

We do not have video

Two independent paired sequences. The gate holding temporal training back is the right call — training on two sequences yields a demo, not a model — but competitively it means all three rivals ship a video product and we ship an image model with a video-shaped hole. Stuttgart HDR alone is 26 videos and 11,336 sequences, and it is what the SOTA paper trains on.

04

ACES is table stakes and we don't speak it

Topaz and Beeble both output ACES 2065-1. We output a normalised 203/10,000 convention that is internally coherent and externally illegible. A Nuke or Resolve colourist cannot drop our EXR into a show LUT without a conversation. This is a small piece of code and a large piece of credibility.

05

Distribution is a ComfyUI graph

Topaz reaches the timeline through a Premiere panel. Runway reaches it through the generator people already pay for. Beeble reaches it through a browser tab. We reach it through a node graph in a tool most colourists have never opened.

06

We have never been measured against anyone

Nobody in this market publishes numbers. That is not an excuse — it is the opening. The SOTA paper hands us public datasets and public CVVDP scores to beat, and we already run the same metric.

04

Where we win structurally

“Structural” is the important word. Topaz could add sliders in a sprint and ACES support in an afternoon. It cannot change what its input is.

Every competitor starts from pixels that have already been clipped. RUDRA can start from latents where the information is still there.

Hyperion, SwitchHDR, and Ruby-on-uploads all perform the same fundamental act: they receive an 8-bit frame in which the highlight information has been destroyed, and they hallucinate a plausible replacement. Topaz's own documentation says so — it infers what belonged in the clipped region. Beeble's founder says so — a plausible HDR representation, not a recovery.

RUDRA's latent path is a different operation. The diffusion latent has not been tone-mapped, has not been clipped, and has not been quantised to 8 bits. Decoding it directly to scene-linear is reconstruction, not invention. That distinction is defensible in a technical conversation with a supervisor in a way that no amount of model quality is.

Three more, in descending order of durability:

Seven open backbones, not one closed one

Runway has native HDR generation for Gen-4.5 and Aleph 2.0. We have HDR-native decoding for Flux.1, Flux.2 Klein, Wan, LTX, SDXL, Qwen-Image and Z-Image — every model an independent studio actually runs locally. Runway will never build this; it has no commercial reason to make competitors' weights better.

Measured radiometry as a product feature

EV-error in stops. ColorVideoVDP in JOD. ΔE2000. Highlight-reconstruction accuracy. Master-display metadata written with real primaries. When every competitor's answer to “how accurate is this” is a marketing adjective, a number on screen is a differentiator — and it is one we are already tooled for.

Client footage never leaves the building

Ruby is cloud-only. For anything under an NDA — unreleased features, talent likeness, pre-release grades — that is disqualifying before the price is discussed. And the price is roughly $30–46 per finished minute at 4K.

05

The position to take

The strategic error available right now is to read the Ruby and Hyperion releases as a challenge to compete on SDR→HDR conversion quality. Do not take it. That is a commodity race against two companies with more data, more compute and a distribution head start, and the winner gets a feature that ships inside somebody else's subscription.

Position RUDRA as the HDR-native pipeline for open models — where SDR→HDR is the legacy fallback, not the product.

Concretely, the one-line pitch is: generate in Flux, Wan or LTX and deliver a graded HDR10 or EXR master without the image ever being tone-mapped to SDR. Ruby cannot do that for anything but Runway's own models. Hyperion and SwitchHDR cannot do it at all — by the time they see the frame, the highlights are gone. It is true, it is verifiable with a probe script, and it is the only claim in this document that a competitor cannot neutralise with engineering effort.

The direct 8-bit path stays in the product, but it stops being the headline. It is what you reach for when the client sends you a ProRes 422 from 2014 — not what the product is.

06

Sequenced plan

Ordered because it is genuinely a sequence — the credibility work gates the differentiation work, which gates the distribution work.

Phase 0This week — nothing else counts until this is done
  1. Run the retrain in the order the fix doc specifies.Re-ingest with the corrected EOTFs, rebuild both manifests, regenerate per-VAE pairs into their own directories, retrain turbo/full decoders with real held-out validation, then Stage 1. Settle the timestep-versus-sigma question against your ComfyUI build before Stage 2 or 3 — it is the only unresolved P0 and it silently poisons both stages.
  2. Freeze all public claims until post-fix weights exist.The Hugging Face model card and the README decoder table describe models trained through the flip bug. Mark them provisional now rather than being caught out by someone who reproduces them.
  3. Fix the multi-crop pair shortage.SDXL, Qwen and Flux.2 Klein sit on 963 pairs against Flux and Wan's 12–13k. SDXL is the paper's own backbone. Regenerating pairs is cheap and is the difference between a defensible Stage 3 and another dry run.
Phase 1Weeks 2–6 — become the only one with numbers
  1. Benchmark on Stuttgart HDR and UBC, publish the CVVDP.The targets are public: 6.56 and 8.91. We already run ColorVideoVDP, so the marginal cost is data preparation, not new science. Whatever the result, publishing it makes us the only party in this market with a verifiable number — including against ourselves.
  2. Run the head-to-head we are uniquely able to run.Same prompt, same seed, two paths: generate SDR then convert with Hyperion 2.5 and with Ruby, versus decode the latent directly with RUDRA. Report EV-error and highlight-reconstruction accuracy on both. This is the demo that sells the position in section 05, and it needs one afternoon of GPU time once Phase 0 lands.
  3. Unblock temporal with the public datasets.Stuttgart alone is 26 independent videos — well past the ten-sequence gate you set. Lifting that gate with legitimate data turns “we have no video” into a shipping temporal refiner, and it costs a download rather than a shoot.
  4. Ship ACES 2065-1 output and an OCIO config.Cheapest credibility in the entire plan. Keep the internal 203/10,000 convention; add the output transform and the config file so an EXR drops into a show pipeline without a phone call.
Phase 2Quarter — take the ground nobody has claimed
  1. Expose the controls Hyperion refuses to.Target peak nits, knee position, per-region EV offset, mask-driven local inverse tone mapping, an explicit highlight-rolloff curve. Topaz has publicly committed to zero sliders; SwitchHDR's masks-and-prompts is the only control surface in the market. Radiance is already a node graph — this is exposure, not invention.
  2. Generate Dolby Vision L1 and HDR10+ dynamic metadata.None of the three competitors does this. We already compute per-frame max and average luminance for MaxCLL/MaxFALL, which is most of the L1 analysis. Shipping correct per-shot dynamic metadata would out-spec every product in this comparison on the one axis broadcast and streaming deliverables actually require.
  3. Leave ComfyUI.A headless CLI first — infer_sdr2hdr.py is nearly there — then an OFX plug-in for Resolve or a Nuke node. The suite stays; the entry point stops being a node graph. Distribution is our worst row in the matrix and the only one that is pure engineering.
  4. Publish Stage 3 with the ablations.The λ sweep and descriptor ablation are short runs, and with Phase 1's benchmark alongside them the paper stops being a description of an architecture and becomes a result. That is what makes the open weights worth downloading.
Stop doing
  • Chasing one-button auto-conversion parity. It is the commodity, and it is where two funded competitors just landed.
  • Quoting decoder PSNR or Stage-1 JOD as SDR→HDR quality. Different task, different input, and a reviewer will notice.
  • Building a credits-metered cloud service. Local execution is the differentiator; metering it destroys the differentiator.
  • Any further UI work on the mockup until it is wired to a real backend or explicitly labelled as a mockup. It is still open from the August fix batch.
