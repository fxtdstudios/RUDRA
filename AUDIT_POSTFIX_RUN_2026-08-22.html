Chimera and the
clipped sun

The image model trained and genuinely improved. Everything around it — the ground truth, the split, the temporal run — is holding it well below what the data could support. Five defects, ranked, with the fixes.

Verdict

Kill the temporal run now. It is still writing as of 11:09 today and every one of its 22 evaluations is worse than doing nothing. The step-0 baseline gate held, so best.pt is still the identity refiner and nothing bad shipped — but that is two days of GPU time spent proving a refiner trained on a single clip diverges.

The image model is real. +3.59 dB log-PSNR over the analytic baseline, best at step 45,000. Keep it, but do not quote it: the held-out set is 96 Poly Haven HDRI panoramas and contains zero video frames.

The ceiling is the ground truth, not the model. Targets are stored as 16-bit linear PNG hard-clipped at 10,000 nits. 78% of the still corpus has a peak above that ceiling — mean peak 6.9 million nits. You are training a highlight-reconstruction model on targets whose highlights are clipped off.

Worth naming plainly: what has been trained is the direct 8-bit path — the commodity lane where Ruby and Hyperion 2.5 already ship. The latent-native decoders, which are the part of RUDRA that no competitor can copy, have not been touched.

02

The temporal run is worse than nothing

P0 · stop now

Every evaluation since step 0 is worse than the frozen image model

The trainer evaluates the refiner against the baseline it was seeded with: the image model alone, refiner at initialisation. That baseline scored log_l1 0.001461, temporal 0.000490. It has not been beaten once in 9,600 steps.

  • Best post-baseline eval — step 1,000 — is 1.95× worse than the baseline on the combined score.
  • Worst — step 9,500 — is 7.4× worse on log_l1.
  • The trend is not noise around the baseline; it walks away from it and stays there.

Root cause is the split, and it is arithmetic. SDRHDRVideoDataset holds out scenes by fraction: n_val = max(1, round(n_scenes × 0.1)). With two scenes in the manifest that is max(1, 0) = 1. So the refiner trains on one Chimera clip and validates on the other. A temporal model with a training set of one continuous shot has nothing to generalise from.

03

The ground truth throws away the highlights

P0 · quality ceiling

Targets are hard-clipped at 10,000 nits — in 78% of the stills

prepare_training_data.py writes HDR targets as clip(linear × 203/10000, 0, 1) × 65535 into a 16-bit PNG. Anything above 10,000 nits becomes code 65535 and is gone.

Sampling 400 metadata files: mean scene peak 6,944,812 nits, maximum 88,170,462 nits, and 77.8% of scenes peak above the 10,000-nit ceiling. On the reference frame I checked (abandoned_bakery_2k, metadata peak 822,026 nits) the stored target tops out at exactly 10,000 — 6.4 stops of highlight range deleted — with 0.24% of pixels sitting on the ceiling.

The consequence is direct: the highlight loss, and the highlight-reconstruction accuracy metric built on it, are partly measuring how faithfully the model reproduces a clip. And SDR2HDRNet is built with max_hdr = 4.0 — it can emit 40,000 nits — so the network has four times the range its supervision does.

P0 · quality ceiling

16-bit linear quantisation leaves the shadows with about 8 bits

Linear encoding spends its code values where the light is. At 0.1526 nits per code, diffuse white (203 nits) lands at code 1,330 and 18% mid-grey at code 239. On the reference frame:

  • 88.9% of pixels fall below code 256 — the entire lower nine tenths of the image shares fewer than 256 levels.
  • 28.2% fall below code 64 (under 9.8 nits).
  • Only 16,491 of 65,536 codes are used at all.

The v2 recipe added shadow opponent-chroma and shadow residual-smoothness losses. Both are supervised against a target whose shadows are quantised into a few dozen steps. The fix is not a loss weight — it is the encoding. Store the target PQ-encoded in the same 16-bit container and the shadows get roughly 12-bit perceptual precision instead. rudra/hdr10.py already has pq_oetf and pq_eotf; this is a write-side change plus a decode in the loader.

04

The corpus is two clips wearing 11,812 hats

P1 · generalisation

92% of records come from two shots of the same test clip

10,849 of 11,812 records are frames of Chimera_DCI4k2398p_HDR_P3PQ and Chimera_DCI4k5994p_HDR_P3PQ. The remaining 963 are Poly Haven HDRI panoramas — one frame each, 965 scene identities in total. Scene-balanced sampling caps video at 25% of image batches, which is the right mitigation, but it cannot manufacture diversity that is not there.

The panoramas carry their own problem: they are equirectangular environment maps at 1280×720, not photographic frames. They are a reasonable source of radiometric range and a poor proxy for the footage the product converts.

P0 · measurement

Validation and test contain no video frames at all

96 val records and 88 test records, every one of them is_video: false. Both Chimera scenes sit entirely in train. So the image model's held-out score — the 42.57 dB log-PSNR — is measured exclusively on HDRI panoramas, while 92% of what it saw during training was video frames it is never scored on.

That number is real and the improvement over the analytic baseline is real. It just does not answer the question anyone will ask, which is how the model behaves on footage.

P1 · measurement

The eval is too small to read

Held-out data is 1.6% of the corpus, and each evaluation samples 32 batches of 2 — 64 crops. The curve swings between 0.038 and 0.064 on adjacent evaluations, which is larger than the gap between the best checkpoint (0.03872 at step 45,000) and the final one (0.04215 at step 50,000). "Best at 45k" is inside the noise floor.

Widen val to several hundred records, raise eval_batches, and fix the crop seed per evaluation so consecutive points are comparable.

05

What went right

Held

The baseline gate did exactly its job

Because best.pt is seeded at step 0 with the analytic-baseline score and only overwritten on a strict improvement, a diverging 9,600-step temporal run produced no shippable regression. This is the single most valuable line of code in the trainer right now — it converted a wasted run into a caught one.

Held

Image-mode splits are scene-safe and honoured

SDRHDRDataset filters on the manifest's own split field rather than re-splitting at random, and the manifest's 965 scene identities do not straddle the boundary. No leakage in the image path — the problem there is composition, not contamination.

Held

The image model beat its analytic baseline convincingly

Log-PSNR 38.99 → 42.57, tone-mapped PSNR 44.10 → 47.89, highlight loss down 40%. On the domain it was scored on, the learned residual is clearly earning its place over the inverse-ACES baseline.

06

The queue

Ordered by what unblocks what. Everything in Phase A is cheap and changes the ceiling; nothing in Phase B is worth running until A is done, because A changes the targets.

Phase AToday — before another GPU-hour
  1. Stop the temporal job.It is still running and has not beaten the baseline once. Keep best.pt — it is the identity refiner and it is the correct artefact to keep.
  2. Re-encode HDR targets in PQ, not linear.Change the write path in prepare_training_data.py to pq_oetf before the uint16 cast and decode with pq_eotf in the loader. Same container, same file sizes, roughly 12-bit perceptual precision through the shadows instead of 256 levels for 89% of the pixels. This is the largest single quality change available.
  3. Decide the 10,000-nit ceiling deliberately.Two coherent answers, and the current state is neither. Either stay display-referred, accept the clip, and stop describing the output as scene-linear and stop reporting highlight accuracy above the ceiling — or raise the normalisation anchor so a 822,000-nit sun survives, and let max_hdr = 4.0 mean something. Write the decision down; it changes what the metrics mean.
  4. Fix the video split arithmetic.n_val = max(1, round(n × 0.1)) gives a 1-clip training set at n=2. Guard it: refuse to build a temporal split below a minimum scene count rather than silently producing one. 
    if n_scenes < 4: raise ValueError(
    f"temporal split needs >=4 scenes, got {n_scenes}")
Phase BThis week — make the numbers mean something
  1. Rebuild the splits so val and test contain footage.Hold out whole clips, not frames, and make video a real share of both sets. Until then no reported number describes the product's actual job.
  2. Bring in Stuttgart HDR.26 independent videos, 11,336 sequences — the dataset the current SOTA paper trains on. It clears the temporal gate with legitimate data, gives you held-out clips, and puts you on the same benchmark as the numbers you would need to beat (CVVDP 6.56 / 8.91).
  3. Re-run the image model on PQ targets.Same recipe, corrected supervision, wider eval. Expect the shadow and highlight terms to move most. This becomes the checkpoint you actually publish.
  4. Report CVVDP, not log-PSNR.rudra/hdrvdp.py is already wired for it, and JOD is what the field compares. Log-PSNR on HDRI panoramas is an internal training signal, not a result.
Phase CThen — the part that is actually the moat

  2. Settle the sigma question, then Stage 1 → 2 → 3.The timestep-versus-sigma units of the second apply_model argument are still unverified against your ComfyUI build, and it silently poisons both Stage 2 and Stage 3. Verify it before spending the run.
