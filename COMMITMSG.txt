capacity ablation: v6 at 64ch confirms the ceiling is the corpus

v6 ran the identical recipe and schedule at --base-channels 64 (4.77 M
parameters against v5's 1.20 M) and reached +1.906 composite against +1.798.
The matched-step curves converge by 80k: v6 leads through the middle (+1.129
against +0.964 at 36-40k) and is level at 76-81k (+1.803 against +1.814). The
extra parameters bought optimisation speed, not capability, and +0.108 dB sits
inside the noise of a maximum taken over 101 evaluations. v5 stays shipped:
equal quality inside the noise, at a quarter the size.

That makes the corpus ceiling a measurement across a 4x capacity range rather
than an inference from a single width, which is the claim the paper needed.

Code review of the previous commit found three real defects, all fixed:

  - Filenames were concatenated into innerHTML unescaped. A frame named with
    an img/onerror payload executed in the page's origin, which can drive
    /api/master. A regression test proves it: the check fails on the old code.
  - Dragging the scrub bar started one uncancelled GPU forward pass per frame
    crossed. There is now a single in-flight request, superseded via
    AbortController, and an aborted fetch is no longer reported as a failure.
  - WebGL uniform locations were looked up on every draw, including inside the
    reduction ladder. They are memoised per program now.

export_bench_pairs.py gained --test-name and --only-test, so a second model is
scored against an existing reference instead of re-exporting 429 frames of it.
Its --condition hard docstring was overclaiming: the degradation model and seed
match the eval, but the eval degrades a 384-pixel crop where this degrades the
whole frame, so the realisations differ. Said plainly now, in both the module
and the README.
